# The Uganda Railway and its impacts

The Uganda Railway (built 1896–1901) connected the Kenyan coast at Mombasa to the interior (then the Uganda Protectorate). Its construction relied on imported labour and had significant environmental, social, and economic effects. The railway facilitated colonial administration, settler access to highland agricultural zones, and the movement of goods and people. It also contributed to contestations over land along its corridor.

Because of its centrality, the railway is frequently discussed in colonial-era economic and political histories of Kenya.
